## Five decisions: what the actual opinions decide

| Circuit | Case | Deadline jurisdictional? | Equitable tolling legally available? | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Third | *Culp v. Commissioner*, 75 F.4th 196 (2023) | No | Yes, presumptively | Tax Court's jurisdictional dismissal reversed; does not automatically give the taxpayers relief |
| Sixth | *Oquendo v. Commissioner*, 148 F.4th 820 (2025) | No | Yes, subject to facts | Remanded for the Tax Court to assess actual entitlement |
| Second | *Buller v. Commissioner*, 160 F.4th 266 (2025), Nov. 26 opinion | No | Yes, subject to facts | Reversed and remanded, not a finding that delay was excused |
| Eighth | *Maniktala v. Commissioner*, No. 25-1366 (Aug. 11, 2026) | No | Yes, subject to facts | Reversed and remanded; tolling entitlement not awarded on appeal |
| First | *Kyick Holdings, LLC v. Bessent*, No. 25-1429 (Aug. 17, 2026) | No | **No**; mandatory filing rule | Affirmed Tax Court dismissal on different grounds; IRS mailing deemed sufficiently diligent |

The First Circuit acknowledges that its tolling conclusion departs from the Second, Third and Sixth Circuits; the Eighth Circuit's August 11 opinion also reaches a different conclusion regarding tolling. The First Circuit discusses the Supreme Court's 2026 *Enbridge Energy* decision while distinguishing claim-processing deadlines from equitable exceptions. This creates a genuine legal dispute about tolling's availability, despite agreement among these five opinions that the deadline is nonjurisdictional.

### Teaching correction and judicial limits

The previous curriculum summary already identified these decisions but compressed different outcomes into one paragraph. The added **APPELLATE-OPINION COMPARISON** now distinguishes three separate questions:

1. Is the rule jurisdictional?
2. Does the statute permit equitable tolling as a matter of law?
3. Did the facts establish entitlement for the individual taxpayer?

A reversal/remand on the second question is **not** a ruling that equitable tolling was granted. A notice mailed to a taxpayer's former address is not necessarily invalid; the address rules, actual record and mailing diligence are distinct issues. No student exercise should tell a taxpayer to file late in reliance on a pending tolling argument.
