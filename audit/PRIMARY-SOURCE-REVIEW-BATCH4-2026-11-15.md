## Pre-Law — Introduction to American Law: `law101/courts`
Expanded the second reading with actual Title 28 statutory sources and a case-based district-court / appellate-court comparison.

Public sources:
- 28 U.S.C. § 1331: https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t28/s1331/
- 28 U.S.C. § 1332: https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t28/s1332/
- U.S. District Courts: https://www.uscourts.gov/about-federal-courts/court-role-and-structure/about-us-district-courts
- U.S. Courts of Appeals: https://www.uscourts.gov/about-federal-courts/court-role-and-structure/about-us-courts-appeals

Narrow findings: ordinary diversity jurisdiction under §1332(a) requires an amount **exceeding** $75,000 (excluding interest and costs) plus applicable party citizenship requirements. Section 1331 addresses civil claims arising under federal law, not federal jurisdiction merely because the defendant anticipates a federal defense. Statutory exceptions, special proceedings and complex citizenship cases require separate review.

## 1L Civil Procedure — `L2-3-1`, Jurisdiction and Pleadings
Expanded the existing hypothetical about a $25,000 Texas/Oklahoma contract dispute into a primary-law analysis distinguishing federal-question and diversity bases, and the Rule 8 pleading requirements from a Rule 12(b)(1) subject-matter dismissal versus Rule 12(b)(6) adequacy of the claim.

Public sources:
- 28 U.S.C. §§1331 and 1332: https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t28/s1331/ and https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t28/s1332/
- Federal Rules of Civil Procedure Rule 8: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_8
- Federal Rules of Civil Procedure Rule 12: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_12

**Source finding:** Rule 8(a) requires a short and plain jurisdiction statement and claim statement; it does not itself create jurisdiction. Rule 12(b)(1) and 12(b)(6) are different procedural defenses. Whether any particular lawsuit satisfies them depends on additional facts and precedent, which were not decided by this exercise.
