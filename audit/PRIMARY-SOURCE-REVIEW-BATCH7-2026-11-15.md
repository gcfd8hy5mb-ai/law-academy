# Law Academy — Primary-Source Review Batch 7 (November 15, 2026)

**Scope:** Four authored 1L lessons: Torts, Criminal Law, Constitutional Law and Property. This is a targeted instructional expansion based on the identified public opinions and enacted law, **not** a state-by-state legal accuracy certification, academic sign-off or professional opinion.

## 1L Torts — L2-2-1: Negligence and premises liability

**Reviewed public opinion:** *Austin v. Kroger Texas, L.P.*, No. 14-0216, Supreme Court of Texas (June 12, 2015): https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/supreme-court/2015/14-0216.html

**Bounded finding:** The opinion addresses certified questions about an employee assigned to remedy a known hazardous condition and discusses invitee premises-liability elements, duty, warning, condition, and causation. A customer slipping on a shifting entry mat is a **different factual setting**, so the lesson asks students to analyze actual or constructive notice, reasonable care, causation, the condition's visibility, and the parties' competing evidence separately. Do not assert automatic liability based only on injury or apply this specific employee-duty holding mechanically to customers.

**Added instruction:** 600-word plaintiff/defendant case analysis, warning/inspection timeline and legal limits of the reported decision.

## 1L Criminal Law — L2-4-1: Criminal culpability and mistake of fact

**Reviewed Texas statutes:** Penal Code chapter 6 (sections 6.01–6.04): https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PE/htm/PE.6.htm ; chapter 8 (section 8.02): https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PE/htm/PE.8.htm

**Bounded finding:** Texas law distinguishes conduct-based intentional and knowing mental states from recklessness and criminal negligence; section 8.02 provides a mistake-of-fact defense under its statutory conditions. A reasonable mistaken belief must actually negate the kind of culpability required; the defense does not universally erase every possible lesser included charge. Neither a defendant's self-description nor a single adverse text message settles the required fact-finding.

**Added instruction:** statutory element extraction, paired prosecution/defense memos, and a decision tree for a wrong-bag hypothetical.

## 1L Constitutional Law — L2-5-1: Commerce Clause judicial review

**Reviewed primary Supreme Court opinion:** *United States v. Lopez*, 514 U.S. 549 (1995), official United States Reports record: https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USREPORTS-514/USREPORTS-514-549

**Bounded finding:** The Supreme Court identified commerce-regulation categories and rejected the particular earlier gun-free-school-zone prohibition as beyond Congress's commerce power. The decision does **not** invalidate every later version of that statute or prove that all local economic conduct is beyond federal power. This precedent must be read in context of intervening and subsequent cases.

**Added instruction:** two contrasting hypothetical congressional acts; authority, standing, and remedy analyses with counterarguments.

## 1L Property — L2-6-1: Estates and future interests

**Reviewed enacted state statute:** Texas Property Code § 5.001: https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/pr/htm/pr.5.htm

**Bounded finding:** In Texas, estate conveyances generally carry a fee-simple presumption absent express limitation or a lesser estate arising by construction or operation of law. A life estate, a remainder and the consequences of a conditional deed remain governed by the entire instrument and applicable state law. The statute is **not** a substitute for jurisdiction-specific interpretation of defeasible estates or the Rule Against Perpetuities.

**Added instruction:** three parallel deed constructions with possession and future-interest diagrams and alternative interpretations.
