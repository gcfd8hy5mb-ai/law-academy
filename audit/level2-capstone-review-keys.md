# Level 2 — Seven capstone substantive review keys
Version: November 2, 2026. Status: **DRAFT FOR QUALIFIED REVIEW — NOT APPROVED**.

This is a grading reference, not a claim that every cited rule is current in all jurisdictions. Compare to the actual lesson, require primary-source verification, and revise as needed. Each capstone: 25 points issue spotting/doctrine, 30 points facts/analysis, 15 points counterarguments, 20 points primary-law accuracy, 10 points writing. A quiz cannot award these points.

## Contracts — L2-1-17
**Fact issue map:** identify which exchanged forms created contract and terms, whether UCC Article 2 applies, consequences of conflicting forms under relevant state enactment of §2-207, installment versus single tender, whether specifications were a buyer condition, seller's delay and partial tender, excuse, demand for assurances and repudiation, cover, incidental and consequential damages, foreseeable lost supermarket contracts and limitation clause enforceability. Distinguish proven figures from claimed figures.
**Required adverse case:** Seller may argue no acceptance of buyer's additional terms, contractual risk allocation, buyer's failure to perform, or mitigation failure. Buyer may argue timely tender was essential, proper cover and reasonable foreseeable damages.
**No-credit error:** State that all commercial forms automatically incorporate every extra term; assume any delay entitles punitive damages.
**Research:** relevant state UCC enactments §§2-207, 2-609, 2-612, 2-712, 2-715 and controlling state opinions.

## Torts — L2-2-17
**Fact issue map:** separate landlord's known deterioration, contractor's obstruction and outage, each plaintiff's status, notice, distinct duty theories, breach, causation, alternative causes, comparative responsibility, physical injuries, derivative emotional injury and recovery restrictions. Build a defendant × plaintiff grid.
**Required adverse case:** landlord argues independent contractor or inadequate notice; contractor challenges causation; plaintiffs identify control, foreseeability and evidence.
**No-credit error:** Treat emotional harm alone as automatically actionable in all states.
**Research:** controlling state premises liability, comparative fault, contribution and emotional-distress decisions.

## Civil Procedure — L2-3-17
**Fact issue map:** subject-matter jurisdiction; corporate citizenship; amount in controversy; removal procedure and timing; complete diversity; forum defendant where pertinent; personal jurisdiction and purposeful contacts; venue; sufficiency of allegations; Rule 15(c) relation back for wrong corporate party; work product/privilege; summary judgment evidence.
**Required adverse case:** defendant contests notice and mistake within Rule 4(m) period; plaintiff presents identity confusion and actual notice. Separate threshold motions from merits.
**No-credit error:** Conclude personal jurisdiction follows automatically from diversity.
**Research:** 28 U.S.C. §§1332, 1391, 1441, 1446; Federal Rules 4, 12, 15, 26, 56; controlling circuit cases.

## Criminal Law — L2-4-17
**Fact issue map:** identify precise charged conspiracy and accomplice statutes, agreement, mens rea, overt act if applicable, withdrawal timing and legal effect, search of digital phone under Riley, arrest and interrogation safeguards, potentially applicable exceptions, evidence admissibility and government's burden beyond reasonable doubt.
**Required adverse case:** defense challenges inference of agreement, evidence scope, custodial interrogation and suppression; prosecution identifies circumstantial agreement and independent evidence. Explain that different conspiracy statutes have different overt-act elements.
**No-credit error:** Claim purchasing tools always proves conspiracy or that all seized phones may be searched incident to arrest.
**Research:** chosen jurisdiction's criminal statutes and controlling constitutional cases.

## Constitutional Law — L2-5-18
**Fact issue map:** whether nonprofit conduct can be attributed to government; distinguish subsidization alone from joint action/coercion/public function; forum nature if state action found; viewpoint versus content restriction; genuine neutral safety restrictions; standing, appropriate defendant, remedies. Independently apply heightened scrutiny to express sex-based grant classifications.
**Required adverse case:** government argues private independent decision or evenhanded safety policy; applicant argues deliberate involvement or discriminatory treatment.
**No-credit error:** Assume any private organization accepting public funds is automatically a state actor.
**Research:** relevant state action, forum speech, standing and sex-classification precedents, statutes where applicable.

## Property — L2-6-18
**Fact issue map:** legal description, record notice and inquiry, adverse-possession elements and state duration, predecessor privity/tacking, utility easement creation/scope and notice, landlord habitability, nuisance, damages, injunction standards and alternative remedies. Record chronological events and a simplified parcel ownership map.
**Required adverse case:** possessor demonstrates continuous open use; owner contests hostile/exclusive use; utility identifies easement source; tenant's landlord raises notice and repair defense.
**No-credit error:** Use Howard v. Kunto's reasoning as the automatic rule for every state or omit the state limitations period.
**Research:** current state property statutes, recorded instruments, controlling state appellate authorities.

## Legal Research & Writing — L2-7-17
**Fact issue map:** isolate the false claim that Erie requires every state procedure in a diversity action; assess federal jurisdiction first; identify whether an on-point valid Federal Rule addresses the specific question; compare Erie and Hanna holdings and their limits; independently check case hierarchy and quotation accuracy; note negative treatment and search dates.
**Required adverse case:** opposing lawyer offers circumstances where an outcome-relevant state law controls; student explains why that does not erase valid federal procedural rules.
**No-credit error:** Invent a pinpoint citation, imply search results prove current law, or confuse binding with persuasive authority.
**Research:** Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64; Hanna v. Plumer, 380 U.S. 460; pertinent federal rule and modern controlling law.
