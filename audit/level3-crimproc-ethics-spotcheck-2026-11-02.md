## L3-7-9 — Confrontation of forensic analysts
Source: Smith v. Arizona (2024), official opinion https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/22-899_97be.pdf
Finding: The Court held that when a substitute expert conveys an out-of-court statement that supports the expert's conclusion only if true, it is offered for its truth for Sixth Amendment analysis. A separate issue remains whether a particular analyst record is testimonial; record purpose matters. Updated lesson to teach both questions and avoid implying all laboratory records are automatically testimonial.

## L3-4-7 — Organizational representation
Source: ABA Model Rule 1.13 and commentary https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_1_13_organization_as_client/comment_on_rule_1_13.ssologout/
Finding: Entity rather than constituents is the organizational client; under paragraph (f), identifying the client is required where the organization and constituent are or reasonably may be adverse, as specified. Reporting up, discretionary disclosures and investigation exceptions have separate conditions under paragraphs (b)–(d). Updated lesson to distinguish the requirements, not characterize all employee interviews as personal representation.
