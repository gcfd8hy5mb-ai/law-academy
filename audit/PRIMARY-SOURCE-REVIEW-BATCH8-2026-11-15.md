## Torts: L2-2-3 — Products liability
Primary legislation: Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code §82.005: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/GetStatute.aspx?Code=CP&Value=82

A covered Texas design-defect claim requires a safer alternative design and producing causation, with the statutory feasibility/utility criteria; the provision has express statutory exclusions. The expanded problem contrasts a manufacturing defect in one kettle with a recurring challenged design in all kettles. It separately prompts warning, product alteration, causation, and evidence research instead of conflating all types of products liability.

## Criminal Law: L2-4-2 — Homicide and intervening events
Primary legislation: Texas Penal Code chapter 19: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/GetStatute.aspx?Code=PE&Value=19 ; chapter 6: https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PE/htm/PE.6.htm

Chapter 19 distinguishes multiple murder theories, manslaughter, and criminally negligent homicide. The new case practice separates mental state and legal causation, with a medical timeline and competing prosecution/defense interpretation of an intervening injury. Texas-specific rules do not automatically apply outside Texas.

## Constitutional Law: L2-5-2 — Speech and sign regulation
Primary opinions:
- *Reed v. Town of Gilbert*, 576 U.S. 155 (2015): https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/576/155/
- *City of Austin v. Reagan National Advertising of Austin, LLC*, 596 U.S. 61 (2022): https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/21pdf/20-1029_i42k.pdf

A facial classification of temporary signs by their message is distinct from an on/off-premises advertising rule that the Supreme Court held was not facially content based merely because someone had to read a sign to determine its applicability. *City of Austin* remanded other scrutiny questions. New hypotheticals distinguish viewpoint-based permit decisions from content-neutral time/place/manner limits rather than treating all speech rules identically.

## Constitutional Law: L2-5-3 — Disparate effect and intent
Primary opinion: *Washington v. Davis*, 426 U.S. 229 (1976): https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/426/229

For the constitutional claim presented, disparate impact alone did not establish discriminatory purpose, although unequal impact can be relevant evidence. The exercise separates facial classifications, purposeful discriminatory administration, mere differences in outcomes, and distinct statutory theories. No modern civil-rights statute applicability is decided without additional inquiry.

## Property: L2-6-2 — Residential tenant rights
Primary legislation: Texas Property Code chapter 92, especially §§92.052, 92.056 and 92.331: https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/docs/PR/htm/PR.92.htm

Repair duties and tenant remedies are conditioned on specified notice, condition, payment, timing, and other statutory facts. Qualifying tenant complaints can implicate retaliation rules, but do not automatically establish liability or authorize rent withholding. The student builds a notice-and-repair chronology and two-sided pleading analysis.

## Property: L2-6-3 — Competing deed purchasers
Primary legislation: Texas Property Code §§13.001–.002: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/GetStatute.aspx?Code=PR&Value=13.001

Texas protects certain later purchasers for value without notice while binding parties and later purchasers with notice in relevant circumstances. Proper recording supplies statutory notice; inquiry and actual notice require separate factual treatment. The exercise varies payment, inspection, possession and deed filing rather than universally favoring first deed or first recording.
