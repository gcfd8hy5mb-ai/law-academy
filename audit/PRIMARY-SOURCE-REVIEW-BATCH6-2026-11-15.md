## 1L L2-3-3: preclusion and final judgments
Sources:
- Taylor v. Sturgell, 553 U.S. 880 (2008): https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/553/880/
- Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp., 531 U.S. 497 (2001): https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/531/497/
Taylor distinguishes claim preclusion from issue preclusion and limits binding persons who were not parties to the prior suit. Semtek raises choice of preclusion law in federal diversity judgments. The new task separates prior claims, parties, actual issue decisions, finality and potential recognized nonparty exceptions. Do not present a universal state-law claim-preclusion answer from these federal decisions.

## 1L L2-3-4: joinder and class actions
Source texts:
- Rule 19: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_19
- Rule 20: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_20
- Rule 23: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_23
Rule 20's transaction/common-question criteria differ from Rule 19's required-party inquiry and Rule 23's class requirements. Rule 23(a) prerequisites alone do not complete the certification analysis; the appropriate Rule 23(b) category must also be met. A damages class under Rule 23(b)(3) requires separate predominance and superiority analysis, together with notice and opt-out considerations. Added a two-sided judicial certification memorandum using the existing 50-consumer scenario.
