Date: 2026-11-15

## 1L contracts — L2-1-1

**Repository issue:** Introductory formation analysis discussed the common-law mirror-image rule and UCC § 2-207 but did not give learners an enacted jurisdiction-specific UCC text or a sufficiently explicit three-subsection decision sequence.

**Sources compared:**
- New York Senate's enacted UCC § 2-207: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/UCC/2-207
- Model UCC § 2-207 reference: https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-207

**Narrow source-supported findings:** Under the published statute, an acceptance with extra terms may form a contract unless expressly conditioned on assent; merchant status, material alteration, objections and limiting language matter to additional terms; recognized performance can also establish a contract. The classroom hypothetical's conditional shipping language does not justify a one-line categorical answer without governing-law and precise-writing analysis.

**Implemented:** Added side-by-side model/enacted-text research and a two-sided formation exercise, including possible performance under § 2-207(3).

**Limits:** No national survey of states adopting Article 2, later changes to particular state versions, or citator review of controlling case construction. This is a statutory-text check, not certification that the hypothetical has a unique correct outcome.

## 3L trial evidence — L4-1-4

**Repository issue:** Existing lesson correctly described Rule 702's 2023 amendment but needed a stronger applied distinction between gatekeeping by the judge and disputes that go to the factfinder.

**Sources compared:**
- Published Rule 702 text and 2023 committee explanation: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_702
- U.S. Courts' current Federal Rules of Evidence index: https://www.uscourts.gov/forms-rules/current-rules-practice-procedure/federal-rules-evidence
- U.S. Courts pending amendments and proposed December 1, 2026 Evidence Rule 801 change: https://www.uscourts.gov/forms-rules/pending-rules-and-forms-amendments

**Narrow source-supported findings:** The 2023 Rule 702 amendment makes explicit that the proponent must demonstrate the Rule 702 admissibility conditions to the judge by a more-likely-than-not standard. Reliability of both methods and application belongs in this threshold determination. A disagreement over the expert's ultimate merits conclusion is not automatically grounds for exclusion; facts/data and method/application must be evaluated distinctly. Pending future amendments must not be taught as already effective.

**Implemented:** Added a structured trial-gatekeeping exercise grounded in the repaired-valve hypothetical and a prospective-amendment verification note.

**Limits:** No review of case-by-case circuit approaches, qualification of this particular fictitious engineer, or state-evidence rules.

## 2L federal tax procedure — L3-5-9

**Repository issue:** Existing lesson addresses an increasingly complicated § 6213(a) circuit split, including equitable tolling. The statute's filing trigger and ordinary time periods should be unambiguously separated from unresolved or venue-dependent tolling doctrines.

**Source compared:**
- U.S. House Office of the Law Revision Counsel, 26 U.S.C. § 6213, current through Pub. L. 119-120 (Oct. 7, 2026): https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t26/s6213/

**Narrow source-supported findings:** After mailing a statutory notice under § 6212, a Tax Court deficiency petition ordinarily has a 90-day period, or 150 days if addressed to a person outside the United States, subject to statutory last-day rules. Section 6213(a) also treats petitions filed on/before the Secretary's specified last date as timely. A preliminary audit proposal is not automatically the statutory notice.

**Implemented:** Added a dated statutory-text checkpoint and cautions against equating a statutory filing period with a conclusion about jurisdiction, late filing or equitable tolling.

**Limits and priority:** The separate circuit discussion involving Culp, Oquendo, Buller, Maniktala and Kyick Holdings still requires opinion-by-opinion checking, subsequent treatment and applicability in the relevant venue. Do not characterize its post-2025 outcomes as independently certified merely because statutory wording was compared.
