# Level 2 Case-Law Companion — Battery and Administrative Due Process

These are historical primary-source case studies to use beside the existing Level 2 Torts lesson “Battery, assault and consent” and Constitutional Law lesson “Procedural due process and administrative hearings.” These are educational analyses, not a determination of current state law.

## Torts — Vosburg v. Putney (Wisconsin Supreme Court, 1891)
**Primary opinion:** https://law.justia.com/cases/wisconsin/supreme-court/1891/80-wis-523-0.html

**Facts:** A school pupil intentionally kicked another pupil, aggravating an existing injury. The litigation addressed the wrongfulness of the contact and responsibility for the resulting injury.

**Predict before revealing:** Must the defendant have intended the full extent of the harm, or can an intentionally unlawful contact support responsibility for unexpectedly serious injury?

**Historical disposition and lesson:** The case is taught for the distinction between intent to make unauthorized contact and intent to cause a particular degree of harm. Evaluate why the classroom setting and absence of consent mattered. Modern battery formulations vary by state; the decision is a historical illustration, not a complete modern rule.

**Writing:** Brief facts, issue, rule, holding, reasoning, and strongest defense argument. Distinguish intentional battery from negligence and assess the effect of consent.

## Constitutional Law — Mathews v. Eldridge (U.S. Supreme Court, 1976)
**Primary opinion:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/424/319

**Facts:** Social Security disability benefits were terminated following administrative review. The beneficiary challenged the lack of a pretermination evidentiary hearing.

**Predict before revealing:** Must the government give every recipient an oral hearing before terminating disability benefits?

**Holding:** The Court held the existing procedures satisfied due process in that context. The analysis balanced the private interest, the risk of erroneous deprivation and likely value of safeguards, and the government's burden.

**Writing:** Apply the three factors to a changed hypothetical in which the outcome depends on disputed witness credibility. Distinguish disability benefits from welfare benefits and other protected interests.

## Accuracy safeguard
For each historical decision, students must verify court, year, procedural posture, later treatment, and the governing jurisdiction before relying on its rule in a current legal problem. The review guide does not automatically award credit or certify legal accuracy.
