# Pre-Law Six-Course Assessment — Reviewer Answer Frameworks
Date: 2026-10-31 · Reviewer-only learning resource · Not a legal opinion or certification

Use this alongside `PRELAW-SIX-COURSE-APPLIED-ASSESSMENTS.md`. These **are not dispositive model answers**. Every example is deliberately under-specified. Grade reasoned handling of uncertainty, not a preferred outcome. Confirm current jurisdiction-specific primary law before scoring doctrinal accuracy.

## LAW 101 — Municipal ordinance and procedural dismissal
**Issue:** Whether the municipality's rule conflicts with applicable state/federal law and whether the case can be heard on its merits. **Analysis:** Identify the ordinance's enabling authority, exact language, claimed statutory preemption, whether the event is protected expressive activity, and the procedural rule underlying dismissal. A dismissal for lack of jurisdiction does not resolve a constitutional challenge's merits. Consider standing, preservation, finality and available appeal. **Opposing side:** The city can argue legitimate time/place/manner regulation or a statutory basis; the association can contest unlawful discrimination or preemption. **Missing proof:** Order, pleadings, local text, event facts and controlling authority. **Scoring warning:** Award no automatic points for simply invoking the First Amendment.

## LAW 102 — Public university and speech forum
**Issue:** Does unequal room access indicate viewpoint discrimination in an applicable forum? **Analysis:** Government action exists for a public university; determine whether a traditional, designated, limited or nonpublic forum best describes the room and reservation policy. Review policies and comparison events; do not infer intent solely from chronology. **Opposing side:** Genuine, consistently applied staffing restrictions may be lawful; viewpoint discrimination claims strengthen where comparable speakers were treated differently. **Missing proof:** Written rules, reservations, correspondence, staff schedules, binding decisions. **Source:** Constitution Annotated / relevant binding opinions. **Scoring warning:** Any claim that all state property is a public forum is mistaken.

## LAW 103 — Lease research and citation
**Issue:** Whether a written notice clause is valid under controlling law. **Analysis:** Identify state, municipality, parties, transaction date, lease text and amendments. Retrieve the official current statute and pertinent judicial construction; check effective dates, amendments, adverse authority, and any local protections. Use commentary only as a research starting point. **Opposing side:** Both landlord and renter may have arguments from contract language, statute and notice facts. **Deliverable:** Research log with publisher, citation, authoritative text location, access date, currentness methodology and unresolved questions. **Scoring warning:** Without jurisdiction, no definitive enforceability conclusion is justified.

## LAW 104 — Retail slip-and-fall
**Issue:** Whether negligence elements are established under applicable premises liability rules. **Analysis:** Discuss duty and visitor status where legally relevant, knowledge/notice of spill, reasonableness of response, factual and proximate causation, injury and supported damages. Analyze possible comparative responsibility as governed by state law, not as a universal bar. **Opposing side:** Employee's knowledge could favor the claimant; store may dispute causation and customer care. **Missing proof:** Surveillance, maintenance records, medical evidence, witness testimony, applicable state law. **Scoring warning:** Injury alone does not establish negligence; phone use alone does not automatically defeat liability.

## LAW 105 — Repair contract
**Issue:** Whether the estimated date, delay, cancellation provision and dispute clause create enforceable obligations. **Analysis:** Read the signed terms, any integration/modification provisions, notice, force-majeure language, governing law and applicable consumer statutes. Distinguish breach and a material breach from delay permitted under the contract. Consider possible negotiation, mediation, arbitration or suit without predicting enforceability. **Opposing side:** Delayed supplies may support contractor explanations; homeowner may have relied on a firm promise or statutory cancellation right. **Missing proof:** Contract, payments, communications, chronology, jurisdiction. **Scoring warning:** Neither cancellation fees nor arbitration provisions are universally valid/invalid.

## LAW 106 — Prospective-client conflicts
**Issue:** What duties arise from consultation and possible adversity to existing client. **Analysis:** Distinguish duties owed to existing client (including confidentiality and conflict duties) from duties owed to prospective client. Under ABA Model Rule 1.18, a prospective client's information can be protected even where no engagement begins; disqualification depends on specific circumstances and potential exceptions. Rule 1.7 raises current-client conflicts. **Next steps:** Limit disclosure, identify both engagements and relatedness, check locally adopted rules, consider informed consent and screening *only if permitted and conditions met*. **Missing proof:** Jurisdiction, exact information, engagement scope, significance and timing. **Scoring warning:** Do not assume model rules control in every state, or that obtaining consent is always sufficient.

## External assessment template
For each subject the reviewer should provide:
- Verified governing jurisdiction, primary citations and review date
- Issue identification (0–15); legal accuracy (0–25); specific application and opposing arguments (0–30); uncertainty/source currency (0–15); organization and conclusion (0–15)
- At least one concrete strength, one error or unsupported claim, and one revision instruction
- Decision: Needs major revision / Needs targeted revision / Sufficient for formative practice / Insufficient evidence to judge
- Reviewer qualification and date

**Completion policy:** A machine-generated topic tally or a student's self-score never substitutes for an independent legal reviewer, and these six practice scenarios alone do not establish readiness for a law degree, academic credit or licensure.
