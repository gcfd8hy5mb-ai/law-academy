# Law Academy — Level 3 (Second-Year Law) Curriculum Blueprint
Status: **Development initiated; not yet integrated into student navigation and not academically certified.**
Date: November 2, 2026

## Transition requirement
Level 2 contains 128 authored lessons and seven capstone examinations. Students may preview the Level 3 syllabus, but this document does not grant Level 2 completion, replace an examination, or establish bar eligibility. Before displaying Level 2 as completed, document a passing software test run and a qualified substantive review with corrected material errors.

## Course sequence and measurable outcomes

### L3-1 Evidence
**Units:** relevance and Rule 403 balancing; witnesses and impeachment; hearsay definitions/exemptions/exceptions; privileges; authentication and best evidence; expert opinion; trial objections and offers of proof.
**Primary law to verify:** Federal Rules of Evidence 401–403, 601–615, 701–705, 801–807, 901–903, 1001–1008 (with state variations).
**Practical case:** Surveillance footage, ambiguous text messages and a witness recounting a third party statement. Students create an admissibility chart, identify hearsay purpose and exceptions, formulate both parties' objections, and draft a ruling that explains discretion.
**Assessment:** 1,200-word motion in limine; 25-question scenario quiz; oral objection exercise.

### L3-2 Business Organizations and Agency
**Units:** agency authority; fiduciary duties; partnerships and LLCs; corporations and entity formation; board and officer duties; derivative litigation; controlling shareholder conflicts; veil piercing; mergers and securities fundamentals.
**Primary law to verify:** chosen state's entity statutes; Delaware General Corporation Law where used, and carefully checked controlling case law.
**Practical case:** A board approves a related-party acquisition following incomplete disclosures. Students analyze process, conflicts, standards of review and available remedies without assuming one jurisdiction's doctrine is national.
**Assessment:** board advice memorandum and 1,500-word litigation analysis.

### L3-3 Administrative Law and Regulation
**Units:** agency enabling statutes; rulemaking; adjudication; due process; judicial review; exhaustion and finality; separation of powers; statutory interpretation after modern Supreme Court developments.
**Primary law to verify:** Administrative Procedure Act, 5 U.S.C. §§ 551–559, 701–706, and controlling contemporary precedent.
**Practical case:** Federal regulator announces a compliance mandate without notice-and-comment rulemaking. Students separate legislative rules, interpretive rules, adjudication, enforcement and available review.
**Assessment:** client advisory and petition-for-review outline.

### L3-4 Professional Responsibility and Legal Ethics
**Units:** confidentiality; conflicts; duties to clients, tribunals and third parties; fees and trust accounts; competence and supervision; advertising; candor; professional discipline; unauthorized practice.
**Primary law to verify:** ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct as a model, then relevant jurisdiction's adopted rules and interpretations.
**Practical case:** A lawyer representing two corporate founders learns of concealed documents and conflicting settlement instructions. Students identify conflicts, permitted disclosure, withdrawal duties and jurisdiction-specific ethics constraints.
**Assessment:** ethics decision memorandum, conflict checklist and supervised oral defense.

### L3-5 Federal Taxation Fundamentals
**Units:** gross income; realization; basis; deductions; capital gains; timing; entities and pass-through structures; administrative procedure; tax research and authority hierarchy.
**Primary law to verify:** Internal Revenue Code, Treasury regulations and current IRS guidance; dates and effective years must be explicit.
**Practical case:** Founder sells a business interest involving installment payments, transaction expenses and stock basis. Students identify unknown facts and build a tax issue map, not a fabricated final liability.
**Assessment:** documented tax research memorandum and supported computation workbook.

### L3-6 Remedies and Advanced Civil Litigation
**Units:** equitable remedies; injunctions and Rule 65; compensatory and restitutionary remedies; declaratory relief; preclusion; settlement design; appellate review and preservation.
**Primary law to verify:** relevant state remedies law, Fed. R. Civ. P. 65, controlling federal and state decisions.
**Practical case:** A former employee allegedly takes confidential customer data and the company requests immediate injunctive relief. Students evaluate standards, evidence, bond, hardship and alternative remedies.
**Assessment:** TRO briefing packet and response, with hearing simulation.

### L3-7 Criminal Procedure and Constitutional Litigation
**Units:** investigations and seizures; arrest and warrants; interrogation; counsel; charging; discovery; plea bargaining; trial rights; sentencing; post-conviction review; civil-rights actions and defenses.
**Primary law to verify:** U.S. Constitution, applicable Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure and controlling cases, distinguishing federal and state systems.
**Practical case:** A suspect is arrested following a geolocation search, interviewed after ambiguous statements, and later contests identification evidence. Students draft suppression arguments and a prosecution response.
**Assessment:** motions packet, oral argument, 1,500–2,000-word case analysis.

## Required lesson design for each unit
Every unit should contain (1) doctrinal explanation; (2) what each legal concept means; (3) why and when the rule applies; (4) case facts and verified procedural posture; (5) two-sided worked application; (6) a problem requiring independent analysis; (7) model reasoning and common errors, revealed after student attempt; (8) knowledge checks and a written exercise; (9) primary-authority citation with jurisdiction and date; (10) explicit warning where current-law verification remains pending.

## Program assessments
- **Mid-course:** researched memorandum with at least three verified primary sources, fact variations and a separately graded counterargument.
- **Course final:** 1,500–2,000-word original scenario and 100-point analytic rubric; automated multiple choice cannot award essay credit.
- **Level 3 integrated capstone:** simulated litigation and transactional problems involving at least three courses, with a source audit trail and oral defense.
- **Completion:** human assessment and corrections recorded; no automatic claim of university credit, accreditation or eligibility to sit a bar exam.
