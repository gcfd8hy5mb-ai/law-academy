# Pre-Law — Six-Course Applied Assessment Pack (Version 1)
**Purpose:** Additional independent practice assignments for all six Level 1 courses. These do not replace the existing 85 lessons or their saved progress. All scenarios are fictional. Do not assign grades based only on answer length.

## Universal analytical rubric (100 points)
- Issue identification and procedural posture: 15
- Accuracy of legal rules and authority: 25
- Fact-specific application and counterarguments: 30
- Jurisdiction, uncertainty, missing facts, and source currency: 15
- Organization and clear qualified conclusion: 15

**Evaluation:** Instructors/reviewers should explain each deduction and identify where a student's source or reasoning fails. This pack does not autonomously grade written answers or confer academic sign-off.

## LAW 101 — Introduction to American Law
**Problem:** Two neighbors dispute whether a city noise ordinance can be enforced against a weekend event. One cites a state statute; the other invokes the First Amendment. A local trial court has dismissed the challenge on procedural grounds. 
**Tasks:** (1) Identify potential sources of applicable law and their hierarchy. (2) List facts needed to evaluate speech claims and local government authority. (3) Distinguish merits from procedural disposition. (4) Outline possible appellate questions without assuming appellate jurisdiction.
**Evidence to seek:** enacted ordinance, state statute, controlling decisions, rules of procedure, docket.
**Primary source starting points:** https://constitution.congress.gov/ ; https://www.uscourts.gov/about-federal-courts/court-role-and-structure
**Common errors:** treating every First Amendment assertion as decisive; assuming dismissal resolved the merits; failing to check jurisdiction.

## LAW 102 — Constitutional Foundations
**Problem:** A state university denies a student organization's request to use an ordinarily open meeting room because of the views expressed at a prior meeting. The university argues that staffing constraints motivated its decision. The record does not establish whether neutral booking criteria were followed.
**Tasks:** (1) Analyze state action and the relevant speech-forum questions. (2) Identify evidence that supports or defeats a viewpoint-discrimination argument. (3) Present both sides' strongest arguments. (4) State the limits on a conclusion until the governing precedent and facts are checked.
**Primary sources:** https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/amendment-1/ ; https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/opinions.aspx
**Common errors:** treating all university property as an unrestricted public forum; skipping intent/evidence; overlooking controlling jurisdiction.

## LAW 103 — Legal Research and Citation
**Problem:** A client wants to know whether a notice clause in a residential lease is enforceable. The lease and property are in an unidentified U.S. state; the client supplies an online article from five years ago.
**Tasks:** (1) Create a research plan that begins with identifying jurisdiction and dates. (2) Distinguish secondary commentary, statutory text, regulation, and binding appellate authority. (3) Keep a source log with title, section/page, publisher, date accessed, and citator/currentness check. (4) Explain why no definitive answer is possible yet.
**Primary-source starting points:** official state legislature and courts for the identified jurisdiction; https://www.law.cornell.edu/ as a secondary starting point only.
**Common errors:** treating a web explainer as controlling law or neglecting subsequent amendments.

## LAW 104 — Legal Writing and Argument
**Problem:** A retail employee reports a spill at 1:05 PM; no warning sign is placed; a customer falls at 1:20 PM. Store video may show the customer looking at a phone. The governing state and admissibility facts are unknown.
**Tasks:** Write an objective IRAC/CRAC memorandum, setting out duty, breach, actual/proximate causation, damages, comparative fault if applicable, and issues requiring discovery. Address the plaintiff's and store's strongest arguments. Do not invent state-law standards.
**Research orientation:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/negligence 
**Common errors:** assuming negligence solely from an injury; treating phone use as a complete defense without jurisdiction-specific law.

## LAW 105 — Everyday Legal Problems
**Problem:** A consumer pays a contractor a deposit for home repairs. The contractor misses the estimated start date but claims supply delays. The agreement contains an undefined cancellation charge and an arbitration clause.
**Tasks:** (1) Identify potential contract, consumer-protection, and dispute-resolution questions. (2) Separate actual contractual promises from assumptions. (3) Identify documents and jurisdiction-specific statutory research needed. (4) Compare negotiation, mediation, arbitration and litigation, without promising an outcome.
**Primary-source starting points:** jurisdiction-specific enacted consumer-protection statutes and contract decisions, verified on official court/legislative websites.
**Common errors:** presuming every late start is a breach or that an arbitration clause is always enforceable.

## LAW 106 — Introduction to Legal Ethics
**Problem:** A lawyer preparing a transaction learns that the prospective new client may be adverse to an existing client on a related matter. The prospective client has already disclosed sensitive information.
**Tasks:** (1) Identify the confidentiality, conflicts, and prospective-client issues. (2) State why model ethics rules are not automatically binding. (3) Outline immediate steps before accepting representation, without disclosing confidential material. (4) Compare potential differences among jurisdictions and explain what must be checked.
**Reference:** https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/
**Common errors:** treating consent as automatic, treating all confidential information as public, failing to identify the jurisdiction's enacted rules.

## Evaluation protocol
For each answer, ask the reviewer to identify: (a) at least two missing facts; (b) controlling law or an explicit need to find it; (c) one substantial opposing argument; (d) a qualified conclusion; and (e) the dated sources actually consulted. Store results separately from existing lesson check completion. A written answer may be marked 'drafted', 'reviewed', 'revision required', or 'externally assessed' only when evidence exists.

## Remaining release gate
These prompts are an **authored assessment resource**, not yet interactive coursework. Integrate as accessible practice tasks, add autosaved student answers and explicit reviewer feedback, confirm legal citations and case currency, and test on mobile/desktop before claiming these assignments are delivered end to end.
