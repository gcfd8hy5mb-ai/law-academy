## LAW 102 — Public university speech / government-controlled forums
**Source:** Congressional Constitution Annotated, *Viewpoint Neutrality in Forum Analysis*: https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/amdt1-7-7-5/ALDE_00013122/ (verify current chapter URL if relocated). Accessible supporting reproduction: https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution-conan/amendment-1/viewpoint-neutrality-in-forum-analysis

**Verified instructional rule:** The level of government control over expression depends on forum classification; the government may impose certain reasonable, viewpoint-neutral subject limitations in a nonpublic or limited-purpose forum, while viewpoint discrimination is constitutionally problematic. Do not assume every university room is a traditional public forum.

**Assessment outcome:** The LAW 102 university-room fact pattern correctly leaves forum status and administrative rationale unresolved. Additional required student analysis: identify the written policy, comparator groups, evidence of neutrality, and governing case law. No substantive finding is justified from the facts alone.

## LAW 103 — Primary versus secondary authorities
**Sources:** Cornell University Law Library, https://guides.library.cornell.edu/legalresearchintro ; Library of Congress Law Library, https://guides.loc.gov/law-secondary-resources

**Verified instructional rule:** Primary law includes statutes, judicial opinions and administrative rules; secondary sources explain and help locate that authority but ordinarily do not bind courts. The federal/state jurisdiction question must be resolved early. Always determine currency and which authority is controlling for the relevant court.

**Assessment outcome:** The LAW 103 lease scenario correctly treats two blog articles as insufficient to decide enforceability. A satisfactory response must provide an explicit primary-law research plan, date-checking method and jurisdiction; it must not pretend the lease can be decided without state law.

## LAW 106 — Consultation, conflicts and professional rules
**Sources:** ABA Model Rule 1.18, https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_1_18_duties_of_prospective_client/ ; ABA Model Rule 1.7 commentary, https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_1_7_conflict_of_interest_current_clients/comment_on_rule_1_7/ ; California's *adopted* professional rules, https://www.calbar.ca.gov/legal-professionals/rules/rules-professional-conduct/current-rules-professional-conduct/chapter-1-lawyer-client-relationship

**Verified instructional rule:** Model Rule 1.18 protects information from certain prospective-client consultations even without a retained lawyer. Conflicts and disqualification require more than simply receiving a call; relevant considerations include relatedness of matters, information significantly harmful to the prospective client, current-client duties, informed consent and permitted safeguards. State-adopted requirements can differ, so ABA guidance is not independently governing law everywhere.

**Assessment outcome:** The LAW 106 scenario appropriately asks students to identify jurisdiction, the existing representation and the significance of information disclosed. Do not award full credit for a generic statement that the attorney must automatically withdraw or that a waiver always cures the problem.
