# Level 2 — Integrated Case Examination and Written Grading Guide
Status: authored assessment framework, not independently assessed student work.

## Exam structure
Seven timed subject-specific writing cases correspond to the current fifth lesson in Contracts, Torts, Civil Procedure, Criminal Law, Constitutional Law, Property, and Legal Research & Writing. Each is a formative law-school style assessment. The existing short knowledge check is **not** a substitute for evaluating the essay.

Students should write a separate IRAC/CREAC analysis for each case, identifying the governing jurisdiction, issues, rules/elements, both parties' strongest arguments, facts requiring investigation, and qualified conclusion. For research and writing, include a dated source-validation log. Suggested independent practice window: 45–60 minutes per case; this is not calibrated to the official bar examination.

## Human reviewer rubric (100 points per case)
| Criterion | Points | Required evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Spotting all material issues | 20 | Identifies primary and secondary issues without inventing facts |
| Rule accuracy and authority | 25 | Accurate elements, exceptions, jurisdiction and verified primary sources |
| Fact application and opposing arguments | 30 | Analytical reasoning addressing disputed facts and best counterarguments |
| Reasoned conclusion and uncertainty | 15 | Conditional conclusions and identified evidence gaps |
| Writing organization and citations | 10 | Clear structure, attributable quotations, usable citations |

**Mandatory feedback:** List the most serious mistaken doctrine or missing element; provide a source-backed correction and a specific rewrite task. A response is **not substantively graded** until an independent reviewer signs and dates the evaluation.

## Reviewer-specific issue flags
1. **Contracts:** UCC sale of goods; repudiation and cover; notice, commercial reasonableness and mitigation.
2. **Torts:** Duty/breach, notice, causation, alternative causes and comparative responsibility under state law.
3. **Civil Procedure:** Diversity, amount, citizenship, removal/remand and possible forum-defendant rule; avoid treating removal as jurisdiction.
4. **Criminal Law:** Intent, the defined attempt test, substantial-step evidence and jurisdictional abandonment defenses.
5. **Constitutional Law:** Government action, forum, viewpoint discrimination, neutral regulatory defenses and comparator evidence.
6. **Property:** Race-notice statute, purchaser value, recording order and actual/inquiry/record notice.
7. **Legal Research & Writing:** Authority hierarchy, currentness checks, accurate synthesis of adverse cases and qualified predictive advice.

## Controls
Results should remain marked **practice/ungraded** until evaluated. Do not automatically award essay scores from keyword presence, word count, or selecting the associated multiple-choice answer. Preserve student drafts on-device; do not import live client confidences into exercises. Keep the dedicated Level 5 held-out simulated bar exam separate from these training materials.
