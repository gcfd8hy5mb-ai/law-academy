# Level 2 — Seven-Subject Timed Issue-Spotting Examination, Version 1
Status: formative examination bank and reviewer guide. Not an official bar exam, academic certification or a calibrated passing score.

## Exam protocol
This is a **new** seven-question integrated issue-spotting set, distinct from the seven lesson-five cases. Suggested time: 140 minutes (20 minutes per question). Complete without outside answer keys, then self-assess with the review guide, which is **not** equivalent to independent grading. A reviewer should evaluate written analysis for legal accuracy, jurisdiction, application and opposing arguments. Keep the question set separate from the held-out Level 5 simulated bar examination.

### 1. Contracts — disputed changes to the purchase order
A retail buyer sends a signed purchase order for 500 chairs at $65 each, delivery by December 10. A manufacturer responds with an acknowledgment substituting December 20 and adding an arbitration term. The buyer does not object and accepts the chairs on December 20 but says late delivery cost a promotional sale. Both parties are merchants. **Task:** Explain why UCC Article 2 may apply; analyze contract formation, additional/different terms, delivery obligations and provable damages. Address counterarguments and applicable state versions of the UCC.

### 2. Torts — premises liability and independent fault
A hotel guest reports a loose stair railing at 8 a.m. Maintenance schedules repairs for 4 p.m. At noon another guest falls, reporting a hand injury. The guest says the railing detached; a witness says the guest tripped on luggage before grabbing it. **Task:** Analyze duty, breach, notice, factual and legal causation, damages, comparator evidence and possible comparative responsibility. Identify evidence to preserve and state-law dependence.

### 3. Civil Procedure — parties in two states
An Illinois citizen sues a Texas citizen in a federal district court in Texas for breach of a $90,000 written contract. The plaintiff also requests an unrelated $10,000 from the defendant's Texas company. The defendant contests personal jurisdiction and venue. **Task:** Distinguish subject-matter jurisdiction, party citizenship, amount in controversy, supplemental issues, personal jurisdiction, venue and possible transfer. Do not assume diversity automatically establishes every other requirement.

### 4. Criminal Law — mistaken ownership
At night an employee enters a storage room, takes a laptop, and places it in a personal bag. The employee says it was a replacement computer assigned to them; a coworker claims they heard the employee say they planned to sell it. The jurisdiction's theft statute requires knowingly taking another's property with intent to deprive. **Task:** Analyze act and mental state, circumstantial proof, possible mistake of fact, and how conflicting evidence affects burden of proof.

### 5. Constitutional Law — campus speech restriction
A state university has a policy allowing registered student clubs to invite speakers into a meeting room on a first-come basis. The university denies one invitation after protests about the speaker's opinions, citing staffing and safety issues. An equally large event with opposing views proceeded the prior week. **Task:** Analyze government action, forum classification, viewpoint neutrality, safety rationale, comparators and possible remedies.

### 6. Property — disputed right-of-way
A deed conveys a northern parcel without express driveway access. Before division, the seller owned both adjoining parcels and regularly used a gravel lane across the southern parcel. The southern buyer now blocks the lane. A public route may be available through costly construction. **Task:** Evaluate easement by implication, necessity, possible prescription, recording and facts affecting scope; distinguish legal doctrines and local rules.

### 7. Legal Research & Writing — supervisory memo
A supervising attorney asks whether a state consumer statute applies to subscriptions automatically renewed after a free trial. You have an outdated online article, a current statute with an exception for regulated industries, and two appellate opinions issued before an amendment. **Task:** Produce an objective research plan and memo outline: issue, jurisdiction, statutory elements, effective dates, negative treatment of decisions, factual gaps, counterarguments, and source log.

## Written scoring (100 points per question)
- Material issue spotting: 20
- Accurate governing law and verified authority: 25
- Application to specific facts and counterarguments: 30
- Reasoned conditional conclusion and further evidence: 15
- Organized writing and accurate attribution: 10

## Review control
Do not call a timed attempt “passed” from completion alone. Record time, student answer, reviewer name and date, checked primary authorities, substantive comments and revision requirements. These cases are educational original hypotheticals; check actual state law before declaring answers legally correct.
