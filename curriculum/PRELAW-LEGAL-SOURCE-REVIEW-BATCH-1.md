## LAW 101: Sources of law and federal supremacy
**Reviewed principle:** The Constitution is the supreme federal law; federal statutes made pursuant to the Constitution and treaties made under U.S. authority have supremacy in cases of conflict with state law. It is inaccurate to say *every* federal statement displaces any state statute. A particular case requires examination of federal authority and whether preemption or a constitutional conflict applies.

**Authoritative checks:** National Archives, Constitution overview (Article VI), https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution/what-does-it-say ; Constitution Annotated, https://constitution.congress.gov/

**Lesson check:** `sources`. Existing multiple-choice explanation properly qualifies supremacy by referring to *valid* federal law and a real conflict. No rewrite needed based on this narrow check. **Further review needed:** jurisdiction-specific preemption examples and deeper coverage of regulatory authority.

## LAW 101: Federal and state courts
**Reviewed principle:** Federal courts exercise subject-matter jurisdiction within constitutional/statutory bounds, while state courts have broad authority over ordinary civil and criminal matters; appeal is generally review for error, not a new trial. Federal jurisdiction has details and exceptions, and appellate review depends on the case and standard of review.

**Authoritative check:** U.S. Courts, About the Supreme Court: https://www.uscourts.gov/about-federal-courts/educational-resources/about-educational-outreach/activity-resources/about

## LAW 102 / LAW 101: Judicial review and Marbury
**Reviewed principle:** *Marbury v. Madison* (1803) is central to judicial review. The Court denied Marbury's requested mandamus in the original-jurisdiction posture, while holding conflicting congressional authorization invalid. Students must distinguish the remedy and original-jurisdiction issue from the broader power of judicial review.

**Authoritative checks:** Federal Judicial Center: https://www.fjc.gov/history/cases/cases-that-shaped-the-federal-courts/marbury-v-madison ; National Archives: https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/marbury-v-madison

**Course assessment suggestion:** Ask students to explain how the Court could find Marbury entitled to his commission but still deny the requested Supreme Court writ; distinguish jurisdiction, remedy, and constitutional review.
