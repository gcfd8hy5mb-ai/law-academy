# LAW 103–104: Research and Legal Writing

## Status
Eight introductory lessons implemented: four Legal Research & Citation and four Legal Writing & Argument lessons, each with explanations, worked hypotheticals, and one formative mastery check. These are **foundational introductions**, not complete J.D.-equivalent courses or rigorous final exams.

## Source and licensing guidance
- [Cornell Legal Information Institute](https://www.law.cornell.edu/): legal research and reference; use links, verify the legal text and terms of reuse.
- [Constitution Annotated](https://constitution.congress.gov/): constitutional doctrine from Congress.
- [U.S. Courts — Court Role and Structure](https://www.uscourts.gov/about-federal-courts/court-role-and-structure): court hierarchy and jurisdiction.
- [U.S. Supreme Court opinions](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/opinions.aspx): authoritative opinions.
- [Harvard H2O Open Casebook](https://opencasebook.org/): open casebooks, with license verification before any reuse.
- [The Bluebook](https://www.legalbluebook.com/): citation manual; do not copy proprietary rules into the app.

## Next content standards
1. Longer source-linked lessons, multiple worked examples and real case briefs.
2. Research logs and user-authored legal memos with clear rubric scoring, preserving drafts on-device.
3. More difficult multi-question assessments and detailed explanations; separate practice completion from evaluated mastery.

This independent educational app neither awards academic credits nor provides legal advice.
